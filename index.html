How this works

This portrait is a fixed 256 × 256 grayscale array. The page displays precomputed matrix product state (MPS) reconstructions from a 16-site, interleaved row/column-bit tensorization. Each setting stores float64 core values; compression is 65,536 original scalar values divided by the number of stored core values. The comparison uses the original pixel values and relative Frobenius reconstruction error; “lower error” means the relative percentage reduction in that error. A matrix SVD is compared only where a rank fits the same scalar-value budget. Neither method includes metadata or file-encoding overhead. These results describe this portrait and ordering, not general performance on turbulent flows.

What I build and test.

Tensor networks · DNS

Compressing isotropic turbulence fields

Question. How much can a turbulent field be compressed before important flow statistics change?

Contribution. I developed MPS, MERA, and PEPS compression and reconstruction pipelines, tested tensor orderings, and evaluated reconstructed fields against DNS.

Scale & checks. 1024³ forced isotropic turbulence fields from the Johns Hopkins Turbulence Database; gradient PDFs, strain-rate invariants, and Q–R joint statistics.

Trade-off. Singular-value truncation reduces stored parameters while reconstruction fidelity depends on the selected ordering and retained ranks. The cited paper reports an a priori encoding study; this is not a demonstrated faster flow solver.

Reacting flows · Scientific computing

Jet-flame statistics workflow

Question. Which turbulent mixing and reaction statistics can be recovered from the jet-flame DNS snapshot?

Contribution. My documented research extends tensor-network analysis to a CO/H₂ jet flame and compares reconstructed fields with LES–FDF outputs. The public repository includes a Python DNS statistics pipeline and a SLURM submission script.

Scale & checks. Source grid 864 × 1008 × 576; the public statistics pipeline uses one 512³ center crop and computes profiles, scalar dissipation, conditional means, and PDFs.

Limitation. Its public README describes the MPS-versus-DNS statistics overlay as a next step, so no such result is claimed here.

Explain my research to…

Turbulence is messy — like swirls in a river or flames in a fire. Computers need tons of memory to describe all that mess. I find clever shortcuts that describe the same mess using way less memory, kind of like folding a giant map so it fits in your pocket.

I develop compression methods that shrink massive fluid simulation datasets by orders of magnitude while keeping the physics accurate. My work combines high-performance computing (Python, Julia, SLURM clusters), advanced linear algebra, and quantum-inspired algorithms, with collaborators at Los Alamos National Laboratory, Naval Nuclear Laboratory, the University of Oxford, and other international quantum physics groups.

I study tensor-network (MPS, MERA, PEPS) encodings of 1024³ DNS fields of forced isotropic turbulence and turbulent non-premixed jet flames, assessing how bond-dimension truncation affects low- and high-order statistics (gradient PDFs, strain-rate invariants, Q–R joint statistics), and explore near-term quantum algorithms for fluid-dynamics PDEs.

Selected publication

2026 · Preprint

A priori Assessment of Tensor-Network Encoding for Isotropic Turbulent Flows

Massen Esmaeili, Hirad Alipanah, Robert Pinkston, Peyman Givi, Daniel Livescu, Andrew J. Daley, Dieter Jaksch, and Juan José Mendoza-Arenas

The preprint reports 99.8% reconstruction fidelity at 5% of original DNS memory for velocity and 15% for a passive scalar. At those settings, total kinetic and scalar energy differ by at most 0.2%; mean dissipation differs by about 10%. The contrast shows why testing gradients and other small-scale statistics matters alongside bulk fidelity. My CV documents developing compression pipelines, evaluating tensor orderings, and validating flow statistics; the paper is collaborative.
