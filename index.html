How the comparison works

Both methods compress the same 256 × 256 grayscale image. MPS uses 16 binary sites, with row and column bits interleaved from coarse to fine, and a fixed maximum bond dimension χ. Its boundary bonds have dimension 1.

CR = 65,536 ÷ stored values. MPS stores ∑ 2χk−1χk values, following Eq. (2.9) of my paper. Matrix SVD stores two factors with the singular values absorbed into one: r(256 + 256) values.

Ranks are discrete. At each slider step, SVD gets the smallest rank with at least the MPS storage budget. Actual CRs are shown separately; this gives SVD equal or more storage.

Error means ‖reconstruction − original‖F / ‖original‖F. Measurements use unclipped float64 arrays; all displayed reconstructions share the [0, 1] grayscale. Error maps also share one fixed scale across all slider positions.

The original 512 × 512 photo is downsampled once with Lanczos. These are genuine, precomputed SVD and TT-SVD reconstructions; the slider selects them without interpolation. CR counts model storage in the same precision, not downloaded PNG sizes.

This photo was selected because MPS wins across several budgets. SVD narrowly wins at the least-compressed setting; neither method is universally superior. These demo results are separate from the paper’s DNS results.

01 / Research

Where physics meets
efficient computation.

01

Tensor networks & reduced-order modeling

Studying low-rank representations of turbulent fields using tensor-network methods, including matrix product states, to understand how physical information is retained under compression.

02

Computational turbulence

Working with direct numerical simulation data and flow statistics, with research spanning isotropic turbulence and turbulent reacting flows.

03

Quantum scientific computing

Exploring quantum algorithms for partial differential equations and fluid dynamics, alongside MPS-based state preparation and variational quantum methods.
